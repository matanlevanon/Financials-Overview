What it does

One page instead of six banking apps

Built for the question you actually ask, which is where the money went and how much is left.

◧

Everything in one view

Cash, savings, credit cards, brokerage and a transaction list side by side. Tiles pack into three columns, transactions hold the right third and scroll on their own.

⌖

Click any row to filter

Every account, category and merchant is a filter. They combine, so one card plus one category shows only that spending on that card. Each block clears on its own.

∑

Numbers that refuse to guess

Net worth subtracts card debt. Card payments are stripped from spending. Cards with no reported limit sit out of the utilisation figure and get reported separately.

▤

Holdings by ticker

Brokerage accounts break out position by position with value, share of the portfolio and gain against cost basis, rather than one lump balance.

◔

Syncs itself, tells Slack

A daily cron pulls fresh data. Optional Slack alerts fire on large transactions and low balances, deduplicated so one event does not arrive five times.

⌂

Your data, your account

Everything lives in your own Cloudflare D1 database. Sign-in is Google, restricted to an email allowlist you set. No analytics, no telemetry, no third-party scripts.

How it works

Three steps, then it runs itself

You need a Cloudflare account, a Plaid developer account and a Google OAuth client. All free.

1

Deploy your copy

Create a D1 database, set your secrets with wrangler secret put, and deploy. You get your own URL on your own Cloudflare account.

2

Link your banks

Plaid Link runs in the browser and handles two-factor prompts. Your bank credentials go to Plaid and never touch this app.

3

Read the dashboard

A daily cron keeps it current. Press Sync now whenever you want it sooner. Enter any credit limit your issuer withholds.

The numbers

Four decisions worth knowing

Most of the work here went into not showing confident numbers that are wrong.

Net worth subtracts card debt

A credit card balance is money you owe. Summing every account into one figure adds your debt to your assets. The headline nets them.

Card payments are not spending

Paying a card off appears twice, once leaving the bank and once arriving at the card. Counted as spending, one payment cycle dwarfs real purchases. Transfers are tagged and stripped.

Utilisation ignores unknown limits

Substituting the balance, or any other stand-in, invents a denominator. Cards without a known limit are left out and the excluded debt is reported next to the percentage.

Pending charges are replaced

When a pending charge posts, Plaid issues a new transaction ID. Ignore that and both copies survive, so every pending purchase counts twice. The sync deletes the superseded row.

Before you start

What you need

Zero cost at one-user scale. Everything below sits inside a free tier.

RequirementNotes
Cloudflare accountFree tier covers Workers, D1 and cron triggers
Plaid accountTrial plan, free, 10 linked institutions, production data
Google Cloud projectOne OAuth 2.0 web client, free
Node and WranglerTo deploy from your machine
Plaid's Trial plan is open to developers in the US and Canada. No business registration, no contract, approval usually automatic. Moving to a paid Production plan is a one-way change you do not need for personal use.
Known limits

What it will not do

  • One user per deployment. There is no multi-tenant mode.
  • US and Canadian banks only, because that is Plaid's coverage here.
  • Plaid's Trial plan caps you at 10 institutions, and removing one does not free a slot.
  • Capital One does not report credit limits through Plaid. Enter those by hand.
  • Chase and Capital One are OAuth banks and need a registered redirect URI.
  • Not a budgeting app. No envelopes, no goals, no forecasts.
